**Explanations to check (English):** French uses the Latin alphabet, but its spelling and its sound are often far apart. It has nasal vowels, an r made at the back of the throat, and a u said with rounded lips. In this lesson six real words show these sounds. Listen first, then repeat out loud. **Silent letters and liaison:** The last letter of a word is often silent: petit is said puh-TEE and Paris is said pah-REE. But when the next word starts with a vowel, a final letter may be pronounced and join it. This is liaison: les amis is said lay-z-ah-MEE. In nasal vowels (on, in, an) you do not say a clear n; the sound comes through the nose. In these lessons a nasal vowel is marked with (n) after it. **Culture:** In French many letters are written but not pronounced, especially at the end of a word, so it is best to learn each word together with its sound. Words like Paris and petit end in a silent letter. People are often friendly and encouraging when a learner tries to pronounce things, so do not be afraid of mistakes.

**Explanations to check (English):** French has two words for addressing one person: tu for friends, family and children, and vous for strangers, older people and formal situations. There are also different greetings depending on the time and the relationship. In this lesson you learn everyday polite phrases. **Tu and vous:** Tu is singular and familiar; vous is singular and polite, and also always the plural. The verb aller becomes tu vas and vous allez. When in doubt, use vous; the other person will often suggest switching to tu themselves, for example On peut se tutoyer ? **Culture:** In France it matters to say Bonjour when you enter a shop, bakery or café, before any request, and Au revoir when you leave. People normally use vous with someone they do not know. Among friends and family la bise, a light kiss on the cheeks, is common, and the number of kisses varies by region and relationship; do not offer it to everyone.

In French writing a comma is used as the decimal mark, so 3,50 € is written with a comma.
