# Tagalog: review sheet for a native speaker

These lessons are a **beta**: written carefully and checked by scripts and a second linguistic pass, but not yet by a native speaker.

**How to review.** For each row: is the target natural and correct? does the pronunciation guide match? do the Arabic, English and Indonesian meanings match? Tick the box when it is right; write the correction in the last column when it is not. Send the sheet back and the lessons are fixed and the Beta label can be removed.

## Please check these first (the reviewers were least sure)

**Stress marks in the pronunciation column** (capitals mark the stressed syllable): kumusta (ku-mus-TA), ospital, botika, tapos (ta-POS), tumawad/tawad, tiyan, tubig, Sabado, dalawa/dalawang, wala, gamot, kape, kasingtaas, Palawan, Maynila (may-ni-LA), Baguio (ba-GI-o or BAG-yo?), upuan, hintuan, libangan, mayroon (may-RO-on), kaunti, kapatid, gulay, kayo (ka-YO), mura (mu-RA), kumakain, i-NOM, ulo, menu, loanwords such as basketball and tricycle.

**Natural phrasing**
- `Pwede po ba ang card?` and `Pwede po bang tumawad?` (shops and bargaining). Would you say `Tumatanggap po ba kayo ng card?`?
- `Pakibigay po ng menu` / `Pakibigay po ng bill` (vs `Pahingi po ng bill`).
- `Masaya akong makilala ka` as "nice to meet you" (vs `Ikinagagalak kitang makilala`).
- `Sampung taong gulang na ako`, `Walang anuman`, `Ingat po`, `Sige po, tingnan natin` (po from a doctor to a patient).
- `Dalawampung piso.` as a jeepney driver's reply (vs `Bente`). `Pwede ako sa Sabado` (vs `Free ako sa Sabado`).
- `Kumakanta kami sa videoke` vs `ng videoke`. `Nagko-commute ako papunta sa opisina`.
- `Magpagaling ka` as "get well soon" (vs `Gumaling ka agad`). `Kita tayo bukas` / `Magkita tayo bukas`.
- `Gusto ko pong magpa-book ng kuwarto` (vs `mag-book`). `Ilang gabi po kayo?` (too short?).
- `Ikalima ng Mayo ngayon` as the date form.

**Grammar and spelling decisions**
- `kasing-` words are written joined and unassimilated (kasingtaas). Should they be hyphenated or blended (kasintaas)?
- `kaysa sa iyon` vs `kaysa iyon`; `kaysa sa` vs `kaysa kay` before names.
- Spellings: kumusta/kamusta, pwede/puwede, kuwarto/kwarto, kasi/kase, baryo/barrio, istasyon.
- Completed forms `bumili` and `bumaba` appear a few lessons before the completed aspect is taught, as ready phrases.

**Facts stated in notes:** 911 as the national emergency number; `salamat` from Arabic via Malay; `kumusta` from Spanish "cómo está"; the look-alikes with Indonesian (lima, kanan, mahal, sakit, tulong, ko/mo ~ ku/mu).

**Explanations to check (English):** Tagalog spelling is very close to its sound, which makes it easy to start. Each vowel is always said the same way, and most words are read as they are written. In this lesson eight real words show the important sounds. Listen first, then repeat out loud. **Vowels, ng and the glottal stop:** Tagalog has five vowels: a, e, i, o, u, and each one is said the same way every time: a as in father, i as in see, u as in too. The letters ng make one sound, like the end of sing, and it can start a word: ngayon. Some words end with a tiny catch in the throat, the glottal stop, which you can hear in the middle of oo. Stress is shown here in capitals: nga-YON. **Culture:** Tagalog is the base of Filipino, the national language, and it is written in the Latin alphabet. People in Manila also mix in a lot of English, and they are delighted when a visitor tries a few words in their language. Do not be afraid of mistakes.

For a respected person we also use kayo (you, polite) instead of ka (you). **Culture:** Filipinos value respect for elders highly, and a well-known custom is mano po, where a younger person takes an elder hand and touches it to the forehead. Using po and opo shows you are polite even if the rest of your sentence is not perfect.

Filipinos like to know your town and your family. They also put Kuya or Ate before the name of someone a little older, as a sign of respect.

**Explanations to check (English):** Text messages are part of daily life. In this lesson you learn the linking words that make your message clear. **Linking words:** Kasi and dahil mean because and give the reason: Hindi ako pupunta kasi may lagnat ako. Kaya means so and comes after the reason: May lagnat ako, kaya hindi ako pupunta. Pero means but and at means and. A comma comes before kaya and pero. **Culture:** Filipinos send many text messages and often end with Mag-ingat ka! They freely mix Filipino and English (Taglish), so you will see both in messages.

**Explanations to check (English):** In this final lesson you bring together everything you learned while talking about a trip to the Philippines: what you did, what you are doing and what you will do. **The aspect map and the markers:** The verb has three states: completed (kumain, pumunta, naglaro), ongoing, made by repeating the first syllable (kumakain, naglalaro), and future, also with a repeated syllable (kakain, pupunta, maglalaro). Welcoming visitors is part of the culture, and the word po opens hearts.
