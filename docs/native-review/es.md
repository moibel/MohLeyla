**Explanations to check (English):** Spanish is largely phonetic: words are mostly read as they are written, and the five vowels (a, e, i, o, u) each have one steady sound. The hardest sounds for many learners are rr, ñ and j. Listen to each word, repeat it out loud, and watch the syllable in capitals in the pronunciation guide, which is the stressed one. **Five steady vowels and a few special letters:** Vowels: a (like "father"), e, i, o, u are all short and clean. The letter h is silent. rr is a trilled r, a rolling of the tongue. ñ sounds like "ny" in "canyon". ll sounds like the English y in "yes" in most regions. j, and g before e or i, is a strong breathy h sound. b and v sound almost the same. Note: c before e or i, and z, sound like s in Latin America but like English th in most of Spain; this guide uses s. **Culture:** Spanish is an official language in many countries, from Spain and Mexico to Argentina, Colombia and Peru, and is spoken by millions of people around the world. Accents and some vocabulary differ from place to place, but speakers understand each other easily. The Spanish you learn here is standard and widely understood.

**Explanations to check (English):** In Spanish the greeting changes with the time of day: "buenos días" in the morning, "buenas tardes" in the afternoon, and "buenas noches" in the evening and at night. You also choose between tú (friends, young people) and usted (older people, strangers, formal situations). When in doubt, use usted; it is the safer choice. **Tú, usted and ustedes:** "Tú" is for someone close: "¿Cómo estás?". "Usted" shows respect: "¿Cómo está usted?", and it takes the third-person verb form (está). For the plural we say "ustedes" (¿Cómo están ustedes?). Note: Spain also uses "vosotros" for the informal plural, but these lessons use "ustedes" because it is used throughout the Spanish-speaking world. **Culture:** In Spain friends often greet each other with two kisses on the cheeks, in many Latin American countries with one, and a handshake is usual in formal meetings or with people you do not know well. Customs vary by country and by person, so watch what others do and follow their lead.

Price words: "caro" (expensive) and "barato" (cheap). **Culture:** Currencies differ from country to country: the euro in Spain, the peso in Mexico and several other countries, and so on. In traditional markets bargaining may be accepted in some places, but in shops with price tags it is usually not expected. Ask politely and do not push.
